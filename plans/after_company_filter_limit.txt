Both plans show the composite index being used, and neither has a Sort node.

-> Company 1006 (zero contacts): 2 buffers, 0.031 ms. The index lookup finds nothing for company_id = 1006 and stops immediately. Before the index, this was the dangerous case: Postgres would walk the entire pkey index looking for matches that didn’t exist.
-> Company 3 (104 contacts): 11 buffers. It reads exactly 10 rows and then stops.

For the same company with the same index, the planner chose a Bitmap scan + Sort without LIMIT, and a plain Index Scan with LIMIT 10. Why does adding LIMIT flip the choice?

Why LIMIT flips bitmap → index scan:

Index Scan returns rows already in contact_id order, one at a time. With LIMIT 10 it stops after 10 rows, so it touches about 10 rows and pages.
Bitmap Index → Bitmap Heap → Sort can’t output anything until it has fetched and sorted all matching rows. With LIMIT, it still does 100% of the work and then throws most of it away.
So LIMIT favors plans that can stop early, and only an ordered scan can.
Without LIMIT, or with LIMIT larger than the estimated matches (e.g. 200 > ~98), there’s no early stop. Every row gets processed either way, so bitmap’s cheaper page access wins.
